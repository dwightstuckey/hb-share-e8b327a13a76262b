Class 01

Habitational

On The Hartford’s slide
  • Modern: 1990 or newer year built
  • Up to six stories
  • Strong occupancy
  • Professionally managed and well maintained
What Erin added
  • Foundation poured 1990 or later. A remodeled 1970s building still doesn’t qualify.
  • Condo risk? Use the habitational condominium class, not commercial condo association.
  • Accurate square footage, garages included.
  • Pre-qualify with your territory manager before you spend time in ICON.

Pricing, deductibles and tiering

Use the pricing tools you already have

1

Compare apples to apples

Wind/hail deductibles often apply on LRO. Line up a fixed-dollar deductible (say $10,000) against a percentage one.

2

Apply credits, name your price

Add your pricing credits on hab and LRO, then tell underwriting the price you need to win.

3

Refer it anyway

If the number looks short on a strong account, tiering may unlock other pricing.

2×

Applying pricing can double your win rate.

From the Q&A

State restrictions and open questions

Answered

How many buildings?

No specific building-count limit. Single-family homes aren’t habitational. In wind-heavy areas, taller (up to six stories) is preferred. Underwriting watches concentration of risk.

Partly confirmed

Which states?

No universal hab appetite. California: hab closed, LRO open. Chris believed Florida and Oklahoma may be restricted for hab; unconfirmed.

Open

Historic register?

The 1990 foundation rule still applies to hab. Erin didn’t know if a 1990-or-newer building can be on the historic register. Ask your TM.

Before you quote: check with your territory manager, or enter the state of operation in EBC to see appetite.

Your next steps

Agent action items

  1. 1
    Pre-qualify hab or LRO with your territory manager when eligibility, state, concentration or details are unclear.
  2. 2
    Hab: confirm the foundation year, use the habitational condo class, send square footage with garages.
  3. 3
    LRO: send a current rent roll with tenant names, operations and square footage.
  4. 4
    Pricing: apply your credits, share your target price, and refer strong accounts even if the first number is short.
  5. 5
    Property managers: look for the comp, and document experience, properties and employee duties.
